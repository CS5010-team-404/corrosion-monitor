# Margin forecaster: training report

Part 1 is the training on simulated data (regenerated by `scripts/train-model.sh`). Part 2 is the evaluation on real
room data and the decision about the bundled model.

# Part 1: Training on simulated data

Model: robust (Huber) ridge regression per horizon on 15 features, predicting the change in margin.
Data: 240 simulated 8-hour runs for training, 60 unseen runs for testing (23400 test points).

| Horizon | λ | MAE model | MAE trend baseline | MAE no-change baseline | Improvement vs no-change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 min | 1e-04 | 0.331 °C | 0.424 °C | 0.452 °C | 27% |
| 30 min | 1e-03 | 0.638 °C | 0.865 °C | 0.796 °C | 20% |
| 60 min | 1e-03 | 1.335 °C | 2.067 °C | 1.528 °C | 13% |

On the minutes where conditions are actually changing (|30-min trend| > 0.02 °C/min, the cases
an operator cares about), the gap to the baselines is larger:

| Horizon | Points | MAE model | MAE no-change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 min | 5654 | 0.401 °C | 0.946 °C |
| 30 min | 5654 | 0.737 °C | 1.553 °C |
| 60 min | 5654 | 0.998 °C | 2.071 °C |

## Early warning classifier: will the margin reach 1.0 °C within the horizon?

Logistic regression, positives weighted 3×, threshold chosen on validation data to maximise F1.
Counted only where the margin is above 1.0 °C now. Many events start with a sudden,
unpredictable failure, so recall can never reach 100% from sensor data alone.

| Horizon | Threshold | Precision | Recall | F1 | Events | Regression-only recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 0.55 | 0.86 | 0.67 | 0.75 | 1262 | 0.62 |
| 60 min | 0.50 | 0.86 | 0.52 | 0.65 | 2330 | 0.36 |

## Warning lead time (what operators feel)

For every moment the margin crosses 1.0 °C in the test runs (43 crossings), how long before it did a warning start?
Rule baseline: warn when the margin is at or below the 3 °C warning threshold (what analytics does without ML).

| | Median lead time | Warned at least 5 min ahead | False-alarm episodes per day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model (60-min classifier or forecast) | 35 min | 88% | 4.2 |
| Rule: margin ≤ 3 °C | 17 min | 100% | 4.6 |

Weights (standardised features) are in the model file. Simulated data cannot prove real-world
accuracy; Part 2 below checks the model on real rooms.

# Part 2: Real-data evaluation (UCI Appliances Energy Prediction)

Protocol: written and committed before any experiment (`docs/ML_FORECAST.md`, "Real-data evaluation protocol",
commit 8a0d591). Code: `edu.neu.ecp.forecast.eval` (harness), `train/UciLoader` (loading and cleaning),
`model/Gbt` (gradient-boosted trees). Reproduce: `scripts/evaluate-real-data.sh` (about 30 minutes on an 8-core
laptop; every number below is reproduced exactly, except the measured inference times).

## Cleaning
Rules (drop, never fill): unparsable rows, duplicate timestamps, impossible values (T outside −40..60 °C, RH outside
(0, 100] %), and a cut wherever two readings are not exactly 10 minutes apart; segments too short for 30 minutes of
history plus 60 minutes of future are dropped. **Result: nothing was removed.** Every location has 19,735 readings in
one gap-free segment. (The rules are still tested on a synthetic fixture: `UciLoaderTest`.)

## Method in brief
- Forecasts only at real reading times; the 10-minute readings are interpolated to minutes only to compute the
  production features from the past 30 minutes (`ExamplesTest.featuresNeverUseFutureReadings` proves no leakage).
- Targets: margin at +30 and +60 min are real readings; **+15 min is interpolated** between +10 and +20 (marked \*).
- Surface = air − 1.5 °C (sensitivity 0.5 and 3.0 °C). Risk event: margin ≤ 1 °C at any reading within the horizon.
- Splits: leave-one-location-out (9 folds) and chronological (first 70 % of time, 60-minute purge). Every choice
  (ridge λ; GBT depth {3, 5} × min leaf {20, 100}, tree count by early stopping, up to 500; warning thresholds) was
  made on the last 20 % of the training period only. Chosen values: `docs/results/chosen-hyperparameters.csv`.
- Candidates: the bundled model unchanged; Huber ridge + logistic retrained on real data; the same on simulated + real
  data; GBT (Huber loss for margins, logistic loss for warnings) on real, and on simulated + real data.
- GBT library: Oracle Tribuo was tried first. Tribuo 4.3.2 (latest) uses xgboost4j 1.6.2, which ships no macOS arm64
  native library ("File /lib/macos/aarch64/libxgboost4j.dylib was not found inside JAR") and, in our linux/amd64 image
  (eclipse-temurin:17-jre), fails without the extra `libgomp1` package. As the plan said, a small plain-Java learner was
  written instead (`model/Gbt`, second-order boosting, quantile bins, depth and leaf limits, early stopping;
  deterministic; tested in `GbtTest`).

## Headline
- The bundled model, trained only on simulated data, **does not transfer**: on real rooms it is worse than "no change"
  at every horizon (LOLO MAE at 60 min 0.931 °C vs 0.441 °C).
- Models retrained on real data are better than the bundled one everywhere, and the best (GBT, ridge on simulated +
  real data) beat "no change" by roughly 5–10 % at 60 minutes.
- For early warning on real data, the simple linear trend is better than every learned model.
- **No candidate met the acceptance rule; the bundled model stays.** The closest (GBT on simulated + real data) failed
  only on false alarms on the simulated test set (8.27 vs 5.60 per day) and on model size (1.84 MB, limit 1 MB).

## Notes on the results
- **Where the events are.** Only the bathroom (location 5) and the outdoor sensor (location 6) ever get within 1 °C of
  condensation at a 1.5 °C offset (238 and 285 events at 60 minutes). In the other seven rooms the warning metrics
  are empty (F1 0.00 with no events and no false alarms), so the pooled warning numbers rest on two locations and
  54 crossings, and the chronological test period (spring) has only 3 crossings. Treat every warning number as
  indicative, not precise.
- **GBT and early stopping.** Several GBT models used the full 500 trees (the grid's cap) at learning rate 0.05, so
  more trees might still help a little; the cap is also why the files are large. Neither was changed after seeing
  test results.
- **GBT warnings on real data alone are poor** (LOLO F1 0.02 / 0.05): the validation period holds very few events, so
  the threshold and tree count chosen there generalise badly. Adding the simulated runs gives the classifier enough
  events (F1 0.22 / 0.18).
- **Moving periods.** While the margin is changing fast (|trend| > 0.02 °C/min), errors are several times larger for
  every model (60-minute MAE about 2.4–2.8 °C, mostly the outdoor sensor). That is where a forecast matters most and
  where all models, including ours, are weakest.
- **Acceptance check (a) was weak.** It compared candidates with the bundled model, which turned out to be worse than
  persistence on real data, so (a) was easy to pass. A future rule should also require beating persistence.

## Limits (read before quoting any number)
- **No real condensation on equipment.** A house is not a server room or a control cabinet, and nothing here observed
  water forming. "Events" are moments when *air* temperature minus an assumed offset came within 1 °C of the dew
  point.
- **Room air, not equipment surfaces.** There is no surface sensor; the surface is air − a fixed offset. A real cold
  spot depends on the equipment, airflow and time of day; the sensitivity run (0.5 / 1.5 / 3 °C) shows the warning
  results depend strongly on this assumption.
- **10-minute resolution.** The model was built for 15-second readings averaged per minute; here short dynamics are
  invisible, and the +15-minute target is interpolated.
- **One building, one season.** One house in Belgium, January to May 2016. Leave-one-location-out shares the same
  house, weather and time with the training rooms, so it is optimistic for a new building; the chronological split
  tests only one spring period.
- **Few events, mostly two locations** (see above).
- **What a real deployment would still need:** surface temperature sensors (or measured offsets per equipment item),
  labelled condensation or near-condensation events from the equipment itself, data at the real reporting interval
  from several buildings and seasons, a per-site check before trusting warnings, and monitoring for drift after
  deployment. Until then, the forecast should support the threshold rule, never replace it.
